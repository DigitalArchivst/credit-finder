# Maps select what we see

A map can be useful without preserving every property of the world. The phrase
"the map is not the territory" captures the difference between a representation
and the thing represented. I use it here as a concise way to explain why the
choice of map matters. I have not supplied a source for the phrase.

The Mercator projection makes high-latitude areas look larger relative to
areas near the equator. It preserves local angles, which has practical value
for some navigation tasks, but it does not preserve relative area. Greenland
therefore looks much larger beside Africa than an equal-area map would show.
