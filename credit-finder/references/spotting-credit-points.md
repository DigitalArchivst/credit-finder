# Spotting credit points

Select material credit questions, not an inventory of every feature. A small work may warrant fewer points than a mode's usual range. Give each point a C-ID and a precise locator.

## Selection

Flag specific quotations, data, factual claims, distinctive methods or forms, named concepts, incorporated material, and existing credits. A named concept needs verification; its name alone does not establish origin. Describe the item before suggesting a creator.

Routine buttons, headings, common transitions, north arrows, ordinary chord progressions, and plain descriptions of the maker's own activity often need no individual historical credit. Record the reasoning briefly. Do not call an item original merely because it is routine or no source was located.

## Text

Inspect quotations, close paraphrases, numbers, named concepts, central arguments, epigraphs, footnotes, and images. Separate the source of a factual statement from a source the writer explicitly identifies as shaping the argument. Record paragraph, heading, page, or line locators. Trace a quotation to an inspected source rather than selecting the most frequently repeated attribution.

## Apps and code

Read README, About, CREDITS, CITATION.cff, NOTICE, license headers, manifests, lockfiles, comments, imports, call sites, bundled assets, public/supplied release history, and relevant network definitions. Distinguish runtime, development, optional, and merely declared dependencies. Do not install or run submitted code just to inspect credits.

Check vendored code and implementations of published algorithms as well as imports. A handwritten routine may share a method without proving influence; a comment explicitly identifying its source may establish more. Date the feature being compared, not merely the product's name. Name collisions can be mentioned neutrally without inferring a relationship or legal conflict.

## Audio and music

Separate composition, lyrics, performance, recording, samples, licensed loops, sound effects, and factual spoken claims. Check liner notes, show notes, supplied cue sheets, timestamps, and declared credits. A source for a recording is not automatically a source for its composition.

A transcript supports speech and lyrics only to the extent transcribed. It does not establish melody, harmony, performance, or an audio sample. If audio cannot be heard, say so and leave those questions unexamined. Do not reconstruct lyrics from memory. Compare only brief passages that were actually inspected and that may be reproduced under the platform's limits.

## Video

Separate footage, still images, music, narration, graphics, captions, animation, and factual claims. Use timestamps and shot/asset locators. Inspect end credits and accompanying records. A caption file does not show the images or establish a visual match. A visual inspection does not establish the soundtrack. Record each unavailable channel.

## Maps

Inspect each layer separately: base tiles, vector features, elevation, boundaries, labels, thematic values, imagery, and overlays. Record provider, dataset/version, transformation, projection, legend, and access date where relevant. Distinguish a map renderer from the data and tile providers it displays.

Keep notices for different layers separate. Check the actual provider or dataset terms, not a generic assumption about maps. Credit a projection's history only when it materially helps; routine cartographic conventions do not need fabricated invention stories. A precise location can be sensitive, so avoid reproducing private records or locations unnecessarily.

## Datasets

Follow original collection, republication, joins, filtering, annotation, coding schemes, schemas, and derived measures. Record dataset version, persistent identifier where present, upstream sources, transformations, and column/table locators. A republisher need not be the original collector.

Distinguish the documentation's claims from provenance actually supported by supplied records. An open access download is not proof of a reuse license. Avoid reproducing personal rows in the report. Summarize the relevant provenance without exporting private data.

## Images and visual design

Separate actual incorporated images, fonts, icons, and templates from resemblance of style, layout, or general chart form. Read captions, metadata, source files, notices, and supplied asset manifests. Shared aesthetics alone do not establish a dependency or direct influence.

For a figure proposed for display in the report, record the exact source, creator, notice, permitted-use uncertainty, and any crop or adaptation. If rights are not established, link to the source and flag the issue rather than recommending reproduction as cleared.

## Good points

- `C01. Numeric claim about population change (essay, Results paragraph 2).`
- `C02. Basemap tile layer (map source, tileLayer call).`
- `C03. Spoken quotation (transcript, 03:12 to 03:18); recording not heard.`

Do not write "C03. The famous technique" without identifying the actual feature and locator. Count excluded conventions only when counted; do not invent an approximate total to fill the report.
